# Presentation evidence register

This workshop explains a public-goods application of the Decentralized Curation framework: personal rewards for honesty, competence and scrutiny can also create public benefit. The live exercise isolates one relevance-scoring step. The handout leads the twenty-slide presentation; this private source register carries technical comparisons and research background outside that sequence. Credible consensus is an intermediate objective, not proof of public benefit. The exercise does not establish that the integrated mechanism is secure or benefits affected people.

## Which source controls which claim

Match each claim to its scope and evidence type, then use the date of the relevant change to resolve conflicting versions. A recent repository push, task update, page edit or publication-metadata refresh does not necessarily change the mechanism or add new evidence.

- **Intended appeal design:** permissionless public crowdfunding of fixed-score positions, including participation by the nominator and ordinary jury eligibility for appellants and backers. Different fully funded scores are counterparties; neither needs to defend the current ruling. A full bounded admission window precedes the shared jury. All funded positions against one ruling combine in one common pot, which covers the shared jury-fee shortfall. The net pot is distributed across funded distinct positions by reciprocal absolute error from the ultimate final score, with a separate exact-match branch. These choices govern the presentation and aligned working manuscript. Published snapshots and inspected code must not override this intent or be presented as implementing it. See [appeal crowdfunding](#appeal-crowdfunding).
- **Curation funding and the distribution budget:** curation costs require funding, while a beneficiary distribution budget is useful upfront but optional. The intended application can curate relative allocation weights before that budget arrives and later apply it to the then-current weights. This author clarification governs the presentation; it does not establish that the inspected contracts already support the separation. Existing nomination-bond, jury-service-fee and dispute or appeal rules still apply. See [score-to-funding interpretation](#score-to-funding-interpretation).

## Judgment and public accountability

The opening asks how private rewards can support useful assessment and correction, and how that work can serve affected people. The [inspected research snapshot](https://github.com/0xferit/manuscript-decentralized-curation/blob/9927927cebd11f3b4e66f80bebee65b3248af919/paper.qmd#L27-L41) requires evidence for both links. Public participation can reveal needs and contribute knowledge; expert or delegated assessment can itself be collective. This is a design aim, not a demonstrated advantage of monarchy over democracy. These sources inform the framing, not claims about the implemented contracts.

## Optimistic accuracy and nomination-scoped outcomes

In the intended design, a factual challenge may begin at any time while the nomination is active, including before its first relevance score and during a relevance assessment or appeal. A withdrawn nomination cannot receive a new challenge. The inspected contracts retain narrower admission conditions; those are implementation evidence, not the intended timing rule. Debunking stops future accrual but preserves all funding earned up to closure as an obligation to that nomination’s beneficiary, including amounts not yet claimed. The nomination bond is forfeited separately; it is not earned funding.

The working paper's Accuracy layer owns this rule: publication requires the normal structural transaction requirements and bond, without prior semantic approval. A successful factual-falsification or non-falsifiability challenge debunks the bonded nomination, forfeits its bond and ends eligibility for future pool funding. Debunking attaches to that nomination, not to a person or all nominations associated with a project. A fresh nomination has its own identity and bond and remains subject to ordinary submission rules; it does not reopen or inherit the earlier nomination's entitlements. The working funding profile no longer permits a reputation threshold to block a fresh nomination solely because an earlier one was debunked. Historical reputation accounting remains separate.

Having necessary material ready for relevance assessment is the nomination's responsibility. The clarification creates no separate pre-draft availability approver and no automatic zero score for inaccessible material. Missing material may establish a violation under the applicable pool policy, but is not by itself proof of falsity. The absence of a successful challenge does not certify either accuracy or evidence availability.

## Earning, claims and nomination withdrawal

In the intended ongoing-funding design, holdback applies only to new nominations before they begin earning. Later rating increases and decreases change future earning when adopted under the ordinary finalization rules, with no new holdback. Once funding is earned, its beneficiary can claim it immediately, including during an accuracy dispute or after the nomination is debunked. An earning condition must not be described as a lock on an already-earned balance. Appeal-backer pots remain governed by their separate chain-finality rules.

Withdrawing a nomination is a separate operation from claiming its earned funds. A nomination withdrawal requested after an accuracy dispute enters the event queue behind that dispute and cannot execute before it settles. If the dispute ends in debunking, that queued withdrawal is cancelled because the nomination has already closed. If the challenge is rejected, the queued exit may proceed under the ordinary exit rules. The inspected contracts' older claim-freeze behavior is retained below as implementation evidence, not as the intended rule.

## Score-to-funding interpretation

The intended application first curates policy-relative knowledge about what matters, represented by relative allocation weights. Curation costs require funding. A beneficiary distribution budget is useful upfront but optional. For effective weights $w_i=\min(\text{requested weight}_i,\text{adopted score}_i)$ among eligible nominations and positive total $W=\sum_iw_i$, the normalized vector has entries $p_i=w_i/W$ and sums to one. A distribution budget $B$ can arrive later and allocate $B p_i$ to each nomination; a funding-flow rate uses the same shares. A zero distribution budget leaves this vector unchanged and creates no beneficiary earnings. Zero total eligible weight leaves no allocatable vector; it does not authorize an equal split. Curation funding retains the existing nomination-bond, jury-service-fee and dispute or appeal rules. This is the author's current design clarification, not a claim established by the historical implementation sources below.

The working paper's Relevance layer owns the interpretation of curated relevance as relative funding weights when an application uses direct proportional allocation. The numeric ratio is a chosen allocation policy, not a measured ratio of public benefit, causal impact or the value of another grant. Each pool's curation policy also decides whether funding need or expected benefit from another grant should affect relevance. The mechanism imposes neither objective and adds no separate need adjustment to the resulting score. Each pool creator authors the rubric and its intended numeric meaning; there is no universal curation policy or new transformation setting.

The inspected funding implementation caps the curated score by the requested weight before normalizing eligible earning weights. The worked example retains that calculation. The working paper's retroactive public goods funding profile instead normalizes scores across surviving nominations under its existing eligibility and holdback rules. The Truth Post feed's relevance-and-confidence ranking, curator voting weights and inverse-distance appeal payouts remain separate. Explaining the funding ratios does not validate the underlying assessments or the allocation's outcomes.

The master paper also makes full allocation with positive eligible weight intentional. A sole positive eligible weight receives the entire distributable amount, even if its score is low; request caps limit relative weight, not absolute payout. This is an intended invitation for other nominations to compete for a share. It does not guarantee entry or independent competition, and it changes no admission window, eligibility condition, holdback, reserve or zero-weight rule.

The example does not estimate the effect of deleting all existing copies, stopping future maintenance or withholding a grant; a pool choosing such a question would need to state its own comparison clearly.

### Appeal crowdfunding

The working manuscript's **Public appeal funding** section is the authority for the intended design. This register summarizes it for the presentation; published snapshots and the inspected implementation retain their own rules. All funded positions against one ruling combine in one common pot, while their individual contribution and backer records remain distinct. The common-pot fee accounting and inverse-distance distribution apply to two or more funded distinct positions. One position is shared by all backers of the same numeric score against the same ruling, on the fixed 0.01 proposal grid; position rounding follows the rule below, while other monetary rounding remains open.

Appeals are permissionless, publicly funded **score positions**. Each position fixes its proposed numeric score and referenced ruling before accepting funds. Anyone, including the nominator, may contribute without approval, curator status or role-based exclusion. An appellant or backer may serve if selected by the ordinary jury draw. No individual can veto other participants' funding.

Every relevance round pins the content revision and its immutable content references before curator reports, and its appeals and retries inherit that target. Where amendments are permitted, publishing revision B does not cancel or retarget revision A's appeal, reset clocks, alter funding terms or release capital. A's jury, forfeiture and zero-funded outcomes remain in A's history and its own chain determines backer settlement. They cannot become judgments of B. B awaits its own assessment under the ordinary hold and resumption rules. The fixed revision preserves the subject of the contest funded by the appellant and opposing score backers. Settlement concerns their positions; authorship alone does not make the author a party to the contest or put the author bond at stake. An author may back a position under the ordinary backer rules. The separate accuracy-challenge rules are unchanged. Until B has its own adopted relevance score, it cannot use any older revision's score for ranking or new score-dependent funding, even as a labeled provisional proxy. Direct links and pending views remain available. An absent score differs from a valid adopted 0.00. Existing score-adoption, state and funding conditions govern once B gets its own score; no new chain-finality wait is added. A rating change creates no new holdback; that condition applies only to new nominations. A's appeal settlement stays separate. Beneficiary funding already accrued under A remains attributed to A and immediately claimable by its beneficiary, including during an accuracy dispute. An edit alone cannot erase it, return it to the pool or move it to B. A reset of confidence does not reset an accrued funding balance. This preserves accrued funding, not merely requested or reserved amounts or provisional batch allocations, whose existing cancellation and redistribution rules remain applicable. The batch profile's submission freeze is unchanged. Pinning content identifiers does not guarantee content availability.

Another fully funded position with a different score is the counterparty. It need not defend the existing ruling: against a current score of 0.60, funded proposals of 0.20 and 0.90 already oppose each other. If the existing score is on the proposal grid, it can itself be proposed and funded, but receives no automatic funded status. Multiple funded proposals are allowed. At most one position can exist per referenced ruling and numeric proposed score. Anyone supporting that score contributes to the existing position while its funding remains open and below the common cap. Numerically equal scores such as 0.7 and 0.70 identify the same position. A repeated submission cannot create another contribution target, payout weight or funding clock. Proposed scores must be $k/100$ for integer $k$ from 0 through 100 inclusive: 101 possible score slots per ruling. Off-grid proposals such as 0.599 or 0.705 are rejected before accepting contributions, without silent rounding. The same numeric score can identify a separate position against a different ruling, including a later appeal level; it still requires fresh funding. Distinct proposal scores are at least 0.01 apart. A funded score cannot be edited; a different score requires a different position.

One pinned pool rule and input snapshot fixes the common positive funding target for every position against the same ruling before admission opens. Proposer, score, filing time, later rule changes and extra contributions cannot change it. A position accepts only the amount still needed to reach that target, using an atomic balance update. A fully funded position accepts zero. Excess stays with the backer or returns immediately without becoming locked or acquiring payout rights. Only accepted funds count toward the target, pro rata shares and refunds.

Accepted contributions lock immediately through the applicable refund or chain-final settlement. They cannot be withdrawn or reused for another position, another appeal level or an ordinary-round seat. The lock survives curator exit and affects only voluntary contributions, not unrelated balances. Penalties outside this appeal cannot consume its accepted contributions. It does not guarantee principal recovery. A position missing its funding deadline is cancelled and returns accepted contributions in full, with no jury fee or right to set the score.

#### Bounded admission and funding outcomes

Every valid issued ruling immediately opens one bounded admission window at its recorded issuance time $t_{\mathrm{issue}}$. The pool-configured positive durations $\Delta_{\mathrm{admit}}$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{fund}}$ are both frozen at issuance. Each duration provisionally defaults to 72 elapsed hours; this is a design choice, not an empirically established optimum. They define

$$
t_{\mathrm{admit}}=t_{\mathrm{issue}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{admit}},\qquad
t_{\mathrm{fund}}=t_{\mathrm{admit}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{fund}}.
$$

Ordinary and appeal rulings use the same timing rule. Opening requires no party action, fee collection or payout/refund claim. Later configuration changes, repeat submissions, funding outcomes and retries cannot extend, shorten or restart these deadlines. Failed-quorum attempts, funding failures and forfeiture outcomes create no new admission window; a new ruling cannot reopen an older ruling's closed window. Both cutoffs are exclusive under the protocol inclusion timestamp $t$: admit a new position only for $t_{\mathrm{issue}}\le t<t_{\mathrm{admit}}$ and accept funding only for $t<t_{\mathrm{fund}}$, subject to the existing position and cap rules. At or after the respective cutoff, that phase is closed even without a transaction recording closure. Signing or broadcasting earlier cannot rescue late inclusion, and same-timestamp transaction ordering cannot extend acceptance. Existing positions may fund at or after admission closure, strictly before their funding cutoff. This convention applies only to appeal admission and crowdfunding.

All positions admitted against that ruling belong to one shared-jury group. New proposals stop when admission closes at $t_{\mathrm{admit}}$. An admitted position can accept contributions from its admission strictly before the common funding deadline, subject to its cap and funding status. Early positions can raise funds during admission; every admitted position has the full $\Delta_{\mathrm{fund}}$ interval after the slate is fixed. This common deadline replaces the separate appellant-fundraising and counterparty-matching clocks.

Two distinct fully funded scores make the group contested, but cannot close admission or start a jury early. After admission closes, the group can proceed as soon as all admitted funding outcomes are known: if every admitted position is already fully funded, there is no need to wait for unused funding time. While any admitted position remains underfunded, the group waits for its funding outcome; at $t_{\mathrm{fund}}$, positions still below their target cancel and return accepted contributions in full. The jury is not drawn or seated until admission is closed and every admitted funding outcome is determined. Failure recognition does not depend on collecting a refund, and neither outcome recognition nor collection resets a deadline.

At least two distinct fully funded scores require one shared jury. The jury assesses the underlying item over the full relevance scale and may issue a score different from every proposal. The 0.01 grid restricts proposed positions only; jury-report, weighted-mean and final-score precision remain unchanged. Settlement uses the actual final score, without rounding it to a proposal slot. An off-grid final score therefore need not have an exact-score position. Additional proposals do not multiply its size or establish independent information. The shared jury earns one actual fee under its frozen ceiling, regardless of how many positions fund. All fully funded positions against the ruling contribute to one common pot. That pot covers the single jury-fee shortfall after the pool contribution; there are no pairwise pots or per-position fee assignments. The remaining common pot is distributed by the inverse-distance rule below; adding a position changes both the common pot and the normalization of payout weights.

At most one ordinary relevance round per item and pool may be scheduled or running, across revisions. Admission, creation and jury-budget reservation are atomic; competing schedulers cannot create a second round or reservation. This guard lasts through valid ruling issuance or cancellation. Issuance opens the appeal hold in the same transition, without a scheduling gap. A permitted ordinary replacement or retry must reacquire admission under the existing cadence, funding and eligibility rules. Other items and pools remain independent, and appeal juries keep their own shared-group and retry rules. The same-item hold on new ordinary rounds begins at issuance and continues through applicable admission windows and active appeals. An appeal ending early does not waive the remaining window. Appeal juries and other items remain eligible; an already-started ordinary round is not cancelled, but that allowance cannot admit a second concurrent ordinary round. For pools with recurring reassessment, the next ordinary round becomes eligible one full configured cadence after the protocol time when the last applicable hold ends. A weekly round due on day 7 and held until day 10 is next eligible on day 17. The old calendar and missed occurrences do not create a catch-up backlog, and a due but unstarted round cannot bypass the new eligibility time. Ordinary funding, eligibility and queue conditions still apply, so this is not a promise of execution. A new applicable hold blocks the start and resets eligibility from the end of the last hold; resolving one group while another remains active starts no timer. Late recognition, closure recording and refund or payout collection do not move the anchor. Already-started rounds may finish, and appeal-jury and failed-appeal retry timing are unchanged. This does not add recurring rounds to the batch profile. Until a valid new jury score or qualifying final score by forfeiture exists for that same revision, its previous score retains its funding role under the applicable revision policy, subject to independent accuracy, eligibility and withdrawal rules. This does not assign an old revision's judgment to new content. Mere retention does not establish finality or release adjudicated funds; the explicit finality conditions below govern, including the zero-funded outcome.

#### Ruling issuance, finality and payouts

A jury issues a ruling by completing a valid round, opening its appeal window and making its earned jury fees immediately withdrawable. Technical round finalization means that completion event. Ruling finality is later: with no admitted proposals, the ruling becomes final at admission closure. If proposals were admitted but none fully funds by the common funding deadline, the referenced ruling becomes final at that deadline and its issued score is the chain's terminal score. No appeal jury is drawn and no new jury fee is charged; all accepted partial contributions become refundable in full. Earlier earned fees remain charged, and earlier adjudicated pots settle against that terminal score with payouts claimable at finality. Neither recording closure nor collecting any refund or payout is required for finality. This creates no new ruling or appeal window and ends only this group's hold; any other applicable window or active appeal still blocks new ordinary rounds. Failed fundraising does not supply additional evidence of correctness.

Every issued ruling, including an appeal ruling, can be appealed again with no fixed depth limit. Each later level references that ruling, uses the established jury-size doubling rule and requires fresh voluntary contributions. Existing locked contributions and reserves cannot be reused. Failed-quorum retries remain within their existing level and do not double its size again.

After earned jury fees are deducted, the remaining adjudicated backer funds stay locked until the entire appeal chain ends. Its ultimate final score determines payouts at every adjudicated level, using each level's own recorded positions and fee-adjusted common pot. Pots from different appeal levels remain separate. Intermediate scores create no fixed or withdrawable backer entitlement. All affected payouts become claimable immediately at chain finality, with no additional wait or requirement that another backer collect first. A later jury cannot charge an earlier level. Later ordinary rounds and separate chains cannot reopen the terminal reference or settlements.

#### An ordinary jury already drawn when the item exits

Under the master paper, an ordinary attempt whose nonempty jury draw completed before final accuracy closure or completed voluntary withdrawal continues against its pinned revision and policy. Its existing deadlines, early-disclosure reporting window, collateral, fee reservation and ordinary participation rules remain in force. Execution order decides whether the draw preceded the exit; preparation or an incomplete or empty draw does not qualify.

A valid ruling earns the normal fee and opens its normal first appeal process even though the item is inactive. The earlier exit is not replayed to cancel that new window for lacking a previously funded contest. Subsequent funding outcomes, fees, appeals and settlement follow the common rules. This permission applies only to the chain arising from that already-drawn attempt; it does not authorize unrelated chains or independent ordinary rounds. Outcomes stay historical and cannot restore status, feed eligibility, beneficiary funding or a returned author bond. Withdrawal eligibility and timing remain unchanged, and authorship alone does not make the author a party.

If the attempt fails quorum, it completes normal proof and failure settlement without a new score, ETH fee or appeal window. Its utility-token collateral and penalties follow the existing rules, and only its unused ordinary fee reservation returns through the normal cancellation path. No fresh ordinary retry may be drawn for the inactive item, even with unused retry allowance. A retry whose own nonempty draw completed before exit still qualifies as an already-drawn attempt. Existing scores and earned entitlements remain unchanged, as do the ordinary retry policies for active items and separate retry rules for protected appeal chains.

#### Accuracy closure or completed withdrawal during an appeal

The master paper applies the same appeal disposition to final accuracy closure and completed voluntary author withdrawal, where that application already permits withdrawal. A request or unfinished cooldown alone does not trigger it. Withdrawal eligibility, cooldown and author-bond return remain unchanged; a relevance appeal adds no wait for its own finality and creates no new withdrawal right.

If any contested level in an ongoing relevance-appeal chain had two or more fully funded distinct positions before either event, that same chain continues under the master paper's rule. This includes either event during a later level's admission or fundraising. The pinned revision, positions, windows, reservations, jury rules and fee terms remain in force. Neither event cancels the appeal jury or its quorum retries, shortens existing windows, or bars further fresh-funded appeals of rulings in that same chain. Funding later levels stays voluntary; the usual zero-funded, unopposed and contested outcomes still govern.

For a chain protected by prior contested funding, neither event imposes a zero relevance score, creates early finality, refunds accepted contributions or releases its reserves. The chain's own terminal score determines the backer payouts. Already-earned fees remain earned and further valid rulings incur the ordinary jury fee; quorum failures remain fee-free. Continued arbitration may therefore spend ETH and prolong locked capital even though the item is no longer active.

Each event retains its existing effects on feed eligibility, confidence and beneficiary funding. Relevance rulings remain attached to the historical item's pinned revision for contest settlement and cannot restore its feed or funding eligibility. This does not authorize a new ordinary round or unrelated appeal chain. If either event becomes effective while the initial appeal process is still open and before any level in that chain has two fully funded distinct positions, stop its proposal admission and contributions immediately. Keep the already-issued relevance score as terminal; do not adopt an unfunded or unopposed proposed score, force zero, draw an appeal jury or charge an appeal fee. All accepted contributions become fully refundable at that event, including a lone fully funded position. Refunds use the existing permissionless collection and standing receiving-address rules without payout rounding. No claimant must wait for another to collect.

Any finality already established under the existing deadlines or outcome rules takes precedence, even if no later transaction has recorded it. In particular, a prior final forfeiture score is not replaced. This cancellation is an explicit exception to the initial admission and funding windows; it is not a general power to alter them. It ends only this process's hold and does not restore a historical item's eligibility.

Evaluate the event and the valid contribution that completes the second distinct position in atomic protocol execution order. Second-position funding first protects the chain; the event first stops later funding, which cannot revive it. Ordinary timestamp cutoffs still apply. Unwind only this cancelled process's unused reservations: unused pool funds return to the pool, while accepted contributions remain reserved for refunds. Do not credit those contributions to the pool, release them twice, disturb another group's reserves or claw back earned jury fees. With a target of 100, positions funded to 100 and 90 refund those exact amounts; if both reached 100 before the event, the continued-contest rule applies instead.

#### Final score by forfeiture

After admission closes and all applicable funding outcomes are known, exactly one fully funded distinct score is unopposed. That score becomes the chain's final score by forfeiture. This is a procedural result, not jury consensus, and opens no new appeal window. Its own contributions return in full, without a jury fee or profit on that position. The terminal score can still change payouts from earlier adjudicated levels. Two different fully funded scores require adjudication because they are counterparties; they cannot both be labeled unopposed. The zero-funded case follows the finality rule above.

#### Reveal quorum

The working paper's Relevance layer owns the fixed quorum rule for initial and appeal rounds: valid, timely reveals must represent at least 80% of the positive weight fixed when the draw succeeds. There is no minimum distinct-curator count. Exact 80% passes; an empty draw fails. With equal-weight seats, 30 drawn seats require at least 24 represented seats, while 12 require at least 10. Later penalties cannot shrink the denominator. One curator holding enough drawn weight can satisfy quorum alone; this threshold measures participation coverage, not independent judgment.

A failed shared-jury attempt produces no new score, jury fee or curator reward subsidy. The contested level remains active and retries at the existing cadence without a retry-count limit or elapsed-time cancellation. Each retry uses its own completed draw for quorum coverage while retaining the level's recorded positions, target, planned jury size, pay rate, fee ceiling and reservations. It neither reopens funding nor requires top-ups. Accepted contributions and reserves remain unavailable for reuse. Existing non-participation penalties, leak-report transfers and releases of valid participants' round locks still apply. The effective score and same-item hold persist, so capital can remain locked indefinitely. Separately specified ordinary-round retry limits retain their own scope.

The paper's existing E2' experiment family uses the earlier five-curator quorum and has not been rerun under this rule; its reported results do not validate 80% quorum availability or adversarial robustness.

#### Separate utility-token and ETH accounting

The master paper's Relevance layer owns the asset rule: a dedicated utility token supplies juror availability stake, incoherence penalties and coherence rewards; other payment flows use ether (ETH), except that successful leak reporters receive confiscated utility tokens directly under the rule below. Jurors receive an ETH service fee and, when available, a separate share of distance-based incoherence penalties in utility tokens. Coherence rewards redistribute those penalties only: no minting or separately funded base token reward. With no distance-based slashing, the additional utility-token reward is zero, while a valid ruling can still earn its ETH service fee. Non-participation penalties retain their separate utility-token pool destination. ETH appeal contributions do not reduce utility-token draft availability, and no conversion is implied. For a valid issued ruling, every timely, valid revealer generates the normal gross ETH fee per originally drawn seat represented by that reveal, independently of coherence, score or dispersion; a proven leak redirects that violator's fee to the successful reporter. Missing, late or invalid reveals earn no service fee; no service fees are earned on quorum failure or another outcome without a valid ruling. Several seats earn the corresponding multiple. Unpaid seat fees are not redistributed to paid jurors; unused reservations follow the existing funding rules. Earned jury fees become withdrawable at valid-ruling issuance, without waiting for an appeal window, chain finality or another participant. A later score change on appeal does not reduce or claw them back. Unclaimed fees stay committed to their assigned recipients outside the fee-adjusted backer pot, and collection does not charge the funding source again. Collection authority follows the recipient-controlled permissionless collection rule below; remaining transfer mechanics are open, and no automatic transfer is required. A proven leak-report reward is funded by punishment of the leaker, without an independent pool subsidy. The reporter receives all utility tokens actually confiscated under the leak penalty and the entire normal gross ETH service fee for that violator in the same round. Multiple represented seats redirect their combined fee. Without a payable fee, including on quorum failure, the ETH component is zero. This is one fee with a different recipient, not a second payment, and does not change the jury bill or refund funders. The presentation calls the master paper's post-reveal proof-opening phase the early-disclosure reporting window. It is the fixed period after the reveal deadline for submitting and validating qualifying evidence of premature score disclosure, before ruling issuance or quorum-failure settlement. The master paper requires this window to have a positive, frozen duration, using the provisional duration defined in that canonical section. Qualifying proofs must be included and validated strictly before the resulting cutoff; otherwise valid earlier openings remain accepted. The new window does not extend vote-reveal or leak-precommit deadlines. There is no early issuance even if all reports and proofs are ready. At the cutoff, new openings are late and issuance or quorum-failure settlement becomes permitted, without automatic execution. Unopened or invalid precommits cannot extend the window or create post-issuance claims. Actual issuance then makes fees withdrawable and starts the appeal clock. Drafted quorum-failed attempts retain their own round collateral and admission hold through the same window before cancellation, without earning an ETH fee. Pre-draw cancellations and empty drafts need no early-disclosure reporting window. Unlocked deposits and other-round collateral keep their existing rules. Apply the existing earliest-valid-precommit rule, redirect the fee at most once per violator and round, and assign its recipient before withdrawals open. No token sale or conversion is involved. Transfers to a cooperating reporter do not establish a net coalition penalty; proof validation and transaction-inclusion limitations remain to be evaluated under that provisional duration. The paper caps the leak penalty at remaining utility-token collateral for the offending round plus currently unlocked deposits, excluding all other active-round locks and amounts already confiscated or transferred. Each token is counted once from an atomic balance state. Insufficient eligible collateral reduces the actual penalty; it does not create debt, require a top-up, alter another round's voting weights or release its backing. The penalty cannot silently enter either the ETH budget or the coherence reward pool. Existing single-asset simulations and reward-padding calculations do not validate this two-asset design.

The master paper rounds each coherent curator's ideal utility reward down to the smallest transferable token unit, once for the curator's entire original locked weight rather than separately per seat. The source is the actual integer distance-based penalties collected for that round. At the existing valid-round reward allocation, the remainder is credited exactly once to that same pool's separate utility-token balance, with no new appeal-chain-finality wait. The bound is one less than the number of coherent recipients in smallest token units, not the appeal grid's position bound. For example, five units shared by two equal-weight coherent curators pay two each and retain one. Three units shared by four equal-weight coherent curators pay zero each and retain all three.

If no tokens were slashed, no extra utility reward or remainder arises. If no coherent recipient exists, all those penalties follow the existing utility-pool destination without division. Returned stake remains separate. Unclaimed integer rewards remain assigned to their recipients and cannot be swept; retained units cannot fund ETH fees or become a new base reward subsidy under this rule. The formula and conservation identity are defined in the master paper.

#### Permissionless ETH collection

Each original recorded recipient has one standing receiving address in each paying contract for its internal ETH jury fees, appeal payouts and contribution refunds, including fees assigned to a successful leak reporter. It defaults to the original recipient. Only that recipient can replace the address or reset it to itself. The setting governs all its unpaid and future in-scope payments, including already accrued amounts, until changed again. The receiving address gains neither ownership nor authority to change the setting. Contract recipients must authorize through their own supported mechanisms; no assumed human controller or protocol operator gains that power.

Anyone may trigger an already-claimable payment to the currently configured address, without another authorization for each payment. Amounts and unlock conditions retain their existing rules. Resolve the receiving address from current state when the payment executes, as part of atomic settlement. A payment executed before an address update uses the old setting and stays final; one executed after the update must use the new setting. Signing or broadcasting earlier does not override this order. Callers cannot use stale instructions or a separate per-payment authorization to bypass the current setting.

Every entitlement is paid at most once, and collection never charges the original funding source again. Failed transfers leave unpaid entitlements reserved and retryable. Updating the receiving address does not itself pay or unlock funds, require a previous failed payment or reopen completed transfers. The original recipient remains the sole update authority. Update validation must reject unauthorized, cross-deployment and superseded instructions; its interface, credentials and replay-protection representation remain implementation work. Transfer-failure isolation and batching also remain open. This rule does not extend to author bonds, beneficiary-registry payments, utility-token rewards or utility-stake withdrawals.

#### Common-pot jury fees

Fresh eligible fee reservations use the pool's unencumbered ETH service budget in successful protocol execution order under the master paper. Each commitment atomically checks the available funds and reserves its permitted share. Earlier filing, readiness, becoming due, signing or broadcasting creates no reservation; invalid attempts acquire none. Existing reservations, earned entitlements, accepted backer capital and other obligations are excluded from the available balance. This does not override the application's ordinary-round order or any admission, scheduling or funding prerequisite. Ordinary rounds still need their full reservation, while appeals cover a shortfall from their own pot without waiting for replenishment. Later deposits and released reserves are available to later commitments, without revising an earlier funding split. Existing appeal retries retain their reservations. Faster inclusion can capture the subsidy and leave an ordinary round without reserved funding waiting; this rule is not a fairness guarantee.

Appeal curators follow the same commitment, reveal, aggregation, reward and penalty rules as initial-round curators. The ETH service budget per drawn seat-ticket is the configurable input; multiplying it by the drawn jury size gives the service budget in wei. Pool-specific configuration is compatible with a suggested reference default. The master paper defines the selected provisional active-review workload for a one-seat curator assessing one item. It is a fee-calibration assumption, not a timer, effort test or extra submission requirement. Multiple seats still multiply the fee without implying proportional research effort. The master paper now specifies the provisional reference ETH fee. Each pool may select its own positive rate at creation, and the reference default changes no existing pool or frozen round. Choosing the default does not establish that it pays for useful assessment or resolve joint calibration with utility-token exposure. The Relevance layer of the master paper defines the actual fee from the number of originally drawn seats represented by timely, valid reveals. It pays each such seat at the fixed rate when a valid ruling is issued, independently of coherence, with a proven leaker's fee redirected to its successful reporter. Returned utility-token principal and redistributed utility-token penalties are separate existing capital, not additional ETH fees. Backer settlement is a separate payment flow.

The common contribution target is $C=\kappa J_{\max}$, with default multiplier $\kappa=2$ and positive fee ceiling $J_{\max}=N_{\mathrm{plan}}p_{\mathrm{seat}}$. Planned jury size and base pay per seat are frozen before crowdfunding. Actual drawn size $N\le N_{\mathrm{plan}}$ gives reward budget $R=Np_{\mathrm{seat}}$; unused capacity is not an earned fee. The actual verifiable fee satisfies $0\le J\le J_{\max}$. Alternative multipliers must preserve $2C>J_{\max}$ after asset-unit rounding; in the unrounded model this requires $\kappa>1/2$. The next appeal's planned seat target is twice the planned target of the valid round that issued the referenced ruling. A smaller actual draw does not reduce the baseline: a round planned for 20 seats but drawing 12 gives the next appeal a target of 40 seats. Extra proposals and failed-quorum retries do not multiply the frozen target again. The target does not guarantee that all seats fill or represent independent curators.

For $2\le m\le101$ fully funded positions with distinct proposed scores, the common pot starts with $mC$. Let $B\ge0$ be the unencumbered ETH service-budget balance recorded at that fee commitment. Before drawing or seating the jury, reserve $U_{\max}=\min(B,J_{\max})$ from the pool and $D_{\max}=J_{\max}-U_{\max}$ from the common pot. No funds can cover another reservation or payout. There is no pool-replenishment wait. When the actual fee is determined, charge the pool $U=\min(B,J)$ and the common pot $D=J-U$, once. Unused pool reserves return to the pool; unused common-pot reserves remain locked until chain finality. The net common pot is $Q=mC-D>0$, because $D\le J_{\max}<2C\le mC$. This is one shared charge, not a separate charge assigned to each position. Per-position and backer records remain necessary for the inverse-distance distribution below.

#### Scalar payouts by inverse distance

Let $s_i$ be the fixed proposed score of funded position $i$, $r$ the ultimate final score of the same chain, and $e_i=|s_i-r|$ its absolute error. When all $m$ errors are positive, the position's ideal total payout, including any returned principal, is

$$
P_i^*=Q\frac{1/e_i}{\sum_{j=1}^{m}1/e_j}.
$$

If one funded distinct position exactly predicts $r$, it receives all of $Q$ and the others receive zero. Evaluate that branch before taking reciprocals; no epsilon or minimum error replaces an exact match. There can be at most one exact-score position against that ruling because all backers of the same numeric score share one position. If it has several backers, they share its payout pro rata by accepted contributions. Repeated submissions of the exact score do not create another exact-match claim.

For integer-wei net pot $Q$, allocate $P_i=\lfloor P_i^*\rfloor$ to each position. At chain finality, credit the remainder $d_{\mathrm{dust}}=Q-\sum_iP_i$ exactly once to the same pool's ETH jury-service budget. Thus $Q=\sum_iP_i+d_{\mathrm{dust}}$. The master paper proves $0\le d_{\mathrm{dust}}\le m-1$, so the proposal-grid cap limits this position-level remainder to 100 wei per adjudicated level. An exact match leaves no remainder. This bound requires the floor of the intended ideal shares, without additional loss from approximate reciprocal weights or intermediate truncation. Unclaimed position allocations remain reserved for their backers and cannot be swept into the budget. The credit does not alter an earned jury fee or reuse a reserved balance.

Each position allocates its integer payout among backers using the master paper's cumulative contribution-interval rule below. A closer position has a larger ideal payout, but can still lose principal after fees; whole-wei allocations can tie or round to zero. Equal positive errors receive equal ideal and floored position allocations. Target and other fee rounding remain open.

For each positive accepted contribution, record its backer and its start and end in that position's cumulative accepted funding, atomically with acceptance. Those intervals partition the fully funded target and retain their protocol acceptance order; repeated contributions by one backer remain separate. Excess, rejected offers and zero accepted amounts create no interval. The paper defines the entitlement as the difference between the floored proportional payout at the interval's end and at its start. Exact product-and-quotient semantics are required; early truncation must not change that result. Later contributions, reordered claims and aggregation cannot change the stored allocation intervals. Collection authority follows the recipient-controlled permissionless collection rule above; remaining transfer mechanics and record representation remain open.

These contribution entitlements sum to the whole position allocation, leaving no second rounding remainder for the pool. Each can be claimed at most once and in any order. Unclaimed entitlements remain reserved, and the last claimant receives no extra balance. Multiple entitlements belonging to one backer add together. Full-principal refund paths return the accepted contributions exactly and do not use this payout rule.

Four equal 1-wei contributions sharing 2 wei receive 0, 1, 0 and 1 wei in acceptance order. Each contribution differs from its ideal proportional allocation by less than 1 wei; adjacent splitting leaves its combined entitlement unchanged. That bound is per contribution, not per backer or person. Interleaving can change the outcome: of ten 1-wei contributions sharing 3 wei, the fourth, seventh and tenth receive 1 wei each. One actor occupying those intervals receives all 3 wei for three-tenths of the funding. This is a conditional allocation example, not evidence of a profitable strategy. Computing one entitlement needs no global backer ranking, but collecting many records is not guaranteed a bounded cost.

For two funded opposing positions A and B, the positive-error formula reduces to the previously selected rule:

$$
P_A^*=Q\frac{e_B}{e_A+e_B},\qquad P_B^*=Q\frac{e_A}{e_A+e_B}.
$$

Distinct opposing scores ensure $e_A+e_B\ge|s_A-s_B|>0$. For scores 0.80 and 0.20 and a net pot of 180, an intermediate 0.70 score suggests 150/30 but creates no withdrawable payout. If the ultimate final score is 0.30, actual payouts are 30/150. Neither proposal needs to equal the previous ruling. At the default target, a maximum fee of 100 requires 200 from each position. With no pool contribution and the full fee spent, the net pot is 300; an exact predictor receives 300 on a contribution of 200. This example neither guarantees profit for a closer inexact prediction nor establishes an optimal multiplier.

For three funded scores 0.20, 0.60 and 0.90, terminal score 0.70 gives errors 0.50, 0.10 and 0.20. Reciprocal-error weights are 2:10:5. A net common pot of 500 therefore has ideal allocations of approximately 58.82, 294.12 and 147.06, respectively. For an illustrative pot of 500 wei, the payable position allocations are 58, 294 and 147 wei; the remaining 1 wei goes to the same pool's ETH jury-service budget at chain finality.

The proposal grid limits how close two distinct positions can be, but adjacent scores such as 0.59 and 0.60 remain legal. Funding several adjacent positions can change the common pot and a backer’s aggregate exposure; each position still requires its full contribution target. The grid does not impose a 0.01 lower bound on error from the actual final score. The profitability and manipulation implications remain an incentive-analysis question; neither the grid nor the formula establishes capital neutrality or resistance to wealth-based attacks.

#### Open specification and evidence limits

Outstanding choices include non-default multiplier calibration; proof validation; update interfaces, replay-protection representation and remaining payout-claim mechanics; target and other fee rounding; and other cancellation outcomes. R03 remains open.

Crowdfunding can assemble capital from several backers, while a bounded admission window gives later contributors a predictable opportunity to enter. Correction still requires timely backing and useful judgment. An unopposed proposal can prevail independently of its merits. Neither the inverse-distance payout formula, sufficient funds nor a larger jury establishes truthful reporting, resistance to every wealth-based attack or eventual completion.

This is an intended-design clarification. The inspected engine instead [collects a prediction stake from the filing address and reserves jury rewards from the pool](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/src/CoherenceEngine.sol#L1593-L1608). Its [successful prediction returns that stake to the appellant](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/src/CoherenceEngine.sol#L1648-L1675); its [losing prediction credits the case's reward pot](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/src/CoherenceEngine.sol#L1713-L1720). Those entry points do not establish the intended public-position settlement. The contract has not been changed here.

### Appellant participation

In the intended design, an appellant may serve on the appeal jury if selected by the ordinary draw. Filing an appeal does not disqualify them, and they follow the same juror rules as other selected participants.

The inspected implementation differs: it excludes tickets registered to the address submitting the appeal. It [records the sender](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/src/CoherenceEngine.sol#L1561-L1574), [subtracts that address's tickets when checking availability](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/src/CoherenceEngine.sol#L1606-L1608), and [removes its ticket entry for the draw](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/src/CoherenceEngine.sol#L785-L793). Other addresses under the same control remain eligible. This is an implementation difference from the intended participation rule, not a defence used in the presentation. The contract has not been changed here.

## Claims the evidence does not support

- **No guarantee that all commitments precede all reveals.** In the inspected [commit and reveal functions](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/src/CoherenceEngine.sol#L939-L975), committing requires a block before the commit deadline, while revealing only requires a block before the reveal deadline and a matching prior commitment. Early reveals can be observed while other jurors can still commit. This is a source-inspection finding, not a reproduced exploit or a claim about a deployed system.

- **No general honesty theorem or safe collusion threshold.** The [published paper, lines 372–396](https://github.com/0xferit/manuscript-decentralized-curation/blob/9927927cebd11f3b4e66f80bebee65b3248af919/paper.qmd#L372-L396) gives a unilateral reward-padding calculation and explains why selected simulation points do not identify a safe threshold. The separate working revision retains the calculation as a single-asset analytical result; it does not validate the selected two-asset rule or complete crowdfunded appeal payoffs. The published link predates that qualification. The implementation's own [majority-bloc test, lines 446–476](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/test/unit/CaseSettlement.t.sol#L446-L476) shows the honest minority being penalized.
- **No established public-benefit optimum.** The manuscript specifies no public-welfare objective function and proves no optimum. Policy fidelity, participant profits and equilibrium would not by themselves establish the best attainable outcome: [lines 668–670](https://github.com/0xferit/manuscript-decentralized-curation/blob/9927927cebd11f3b4e66f80bebee65b3248af919/paper.qmd#L668-L670).
- **No guarantee of consistency across claims.** Separate challenge outcomes do not establish that all retained claims can be true together. The protocol specifies no cross-item consistency test: [lines 672–674](https://github.com/0xferit/manuscript-decentralized-curation/blob/9927927cebd11f3b4e66f80bebee65b3248af919/paper.qmd#L672-L674).
- **No verified production deployment or completed audit of this integrated implementation.** This review establishes source content, not operational status. The paper reports [a partial accuracy-layer deployment in 2023](https://github.com/0xferit/manuscript-decentralized-curation/blob/9927927cebd11f3b4e66f80bebee65b3248af919/paper.qmd#L23); that is a different claim.
- **No unlimited-jury guarantee.** No configured appeal-depth ceiling exists, but the implementation documents [a finite seat-field operating bound](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/src/CoherenceEngine.sol#L291-L311).
- **No automatic elimination of trust.** Pool terms disclose the assets, arbitrator and paymaster. A configured temporary circuit breaker is an [express fairness exception](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/src/Pool.sol#L1734-L1763).
- **No implemented juror leak penalty established here.** The workshop's disclosure bounty and the news blueprint's proposed reporting path must not be attributed to the inspected Solidity engine.
- **No implemented curator stipend in this release.** It is [explicitly deferred](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-15-dispute-coherence-design.md#L71-L77).

For facilitation: present the loop first, identify what the exercise isolates, then distinguish observations from hypotheses. Agreement, disagreement, a successful appeal example or a smooth workshop run each answers a narrower question than whether the full system works.
