# Facilitator guide

Run this as an eighty-minute workshop about the decentralized curation thesis and its aim of turning private incentives into credible consensus and public benefit. Public-goods funding is the application explored here. The opening worked example curates relative allocation shares from adjustable scores for a real contribution, then applies an optional funding flow. The later live jury examines one component; neither activity simulates the entire protocol. Times run from the session start. Slides 21 to 24 are optional references.

For rehearsal and delivery, use the [slide-by-slide speaker script](speaker-script.md) and [printable speech](../public/speech/speech.pdf). The projected deck contains visual prompts; the script carries the explanation and optional detail. The [offline deck](../public/deck/deck.pdf) is a fallback when the live demonstration is unavailable.

## Before people arrive

- Print the [handout](../public/handout/handout.pdf) on A4 paper and the [score sheets](../public/paper/sheets.html), two A5 sheets per A4 page. For thirty participants, print thirty handouts and fifteen pages of score sheets. Scan all three handout codes and check that the room, working paper and mailing list open.
- Rehearse joining, sealing, automatic opening, grading and host takeover in a separate room on a laptop and phone. Keep the session room for the session.
- Open the [deck](../public/deck/index.html) and separate host controls. Press `F1` or select **Shortcuts** for the keyboard guide, including next, previous, first and last slide controls. Charge both devices and check Wi-Fi and mobile data.
- Rehearse the NumPy example on slides 4 to 10: change a curator score, apply the appeal, set grant flow to zero and restore it, show the debunked branch, apply the lower re-score and reset. These controls share one state; return to the allocation table on slide 8 after each change. Reset before the session.
- Without a shared display, direct participants to the deck from the home page, announce slide numbers and read the policy and results aloud. Let participants return to the game before opening a nominee.
- Review [likely questions](likely-questions.md), the audience-facing [mechanism summary](../public/mechanism/index.html), and the [evaluation plan](evaluation-plan.md). Keep the paper, inspected contracts, workshop and Arrow distinct.
- Before participants arrive, privately record the comparison you expect between the portable document format round and the other rounds. Keep that note and the expected direction off participant-facing screens and out of the briefing until all compared rounds have closed. Do not call it preregistered research unless a dated record was actually fixed before scoring.

Summon the jury from the laptop: enter an alias, keep the six default nominees selected and press “Summon a jury and host it”. Leave the spares unticked; the docket cannot change after summoning. Keep the lobby open until the joining segment.

## Explain the thesis, then the funding application

Open with: “Research depends on judgments about what to read, cite, reuse and reward. Those judgments take work, and incentives shape how that work is done. My thesis asks whether private incentives can make careful assessment and useful challenges worthwhile, helping us build credible collective judgments.”

State the scope: “Credible consensus is the general design problem. Today we explore public-goods funding as one application.”

Use the conference memo to tailor examples and language to the research audience. The author has worked on this thesis since 2021; do not present it as an idea conceived at the conference. The memo is not a survey or evidence of demand, adoption or efficacy. Acknowledge preservation, discovery and granular attribution as distinct parts of the research infrastructure. This mechanism depends on accessible evidence; it does not supply those other layers by itself. Explain the relationship between metrics and judgment: choosing a measure, its weight and its interpretation embeds a purpose. Metrics can help, and a jury score can also be gamed. The relevant question is how assessment can be inspected, challenged and improved.

Use Appendix C when participants ask about adjacent work. The assessment workshop makes funder goals and evidence weights inspectable; The Unjournal already pays for public expert evaluation; the Open Source Endowment combines published measures with human review and board approval; Matadisco supports discovery. Present these as complementary approaches, not failed competitors or confirmed partners. The distinction to investigate is the proposed organization of participation, incentives and correction. See the [conference context and source checks](presentation-sources.md#conference-observations-and-adjacent-work).


Then name the intermediate objective: “Credible consensus: a collective judgment people have reason to rely on. The final aim is public benefit, not agreement for its own sake or a claim to certify truth.” The [research framing](presentation-sources.md#judgment-and-public-accountability) supports combining informed judgment with participation and scrutiny. It does not establish that monarchy produces better decisions or that democracy only counts preferences.

Explain what credibility requires: inspectable evidence, a public policy, incentives for careful judgment, and usable routes to challenge and revision. Consensus means the resulting collective judgment, not unanimity. Accuracy challenges still address false factual claims; relevance judgments remain relative to the pool’s policy. These mechanisms aim to earn credibility, and agreement alone does not demonstrate success. Keep two tests separate: do private rewards induce useful work, and does that work improve decisions for the people affected?

Use the NumPy 2.0.0 release throughout. Say: “The release and source records are real. This nomination, its curator scores and its funding parameters are illustrative. No NumPy application, payment or accuracy ruling is being reported.” The shared policy scores the nominee; the release is evidence about its work, not the entire object being scored.

- **Slides 1 to 3:** Separate who assesses a contribution from whose needs the assessment serves. Then name the application's complete loop: the creator states the policy; jurors assess relevance; wider participants can supply evidence, fund eligible challenges and crowdfund relevance appeals; resulting weights describe relative importance under that policy. Grant funding can arrive later and apply to those shares. Bonds and jury costs still require their own funding; an unopposed funded position returns its contributions in full without a jury fee. This is delegated assessment with correction paths, not a guarantee that everyone is represented. Return to the map whenever a component is mistaken for the whole system.
- **Slides 4 and 5:** Read the scoring prompt and anchors generated from the shared policy. The creator chooses the policy; publishing it makes the criterion inspectable, not democratically authorized. The pool fixes economic terms but need not start with a beneficiary grant budget; the prompt states what curators score and the rubric guides how they score it. Inspect the release announcement, delivered capabilities, SciPy's preparation for compatibility, and NumPy's migration costs. A nomination identifies a beneficiary, supplies evidence, requests an applied weight and posts a deposit in the funding asset. A requested weight is a ceiling, not a promised share.
- **Slide 6:** Move a curator's score and inspect the weighted mean. Six drawn tickets belong to three illustrative curators: stake buys potential seats, not certified expertise or six independent people. These controls represent revealed reports; they do not enact commit and reveal. The current contract binds each reveal to a commitment but accepts early reveals while other jurors can still commit. Do not promise that it prevents score copying. Reset the example before showing the baseline below.
- **Slide 7:** Apply the twelve-ticket appeal result. In this adjudicated example, the final jury’s mean supplies the adopted relevance score; its median and median absolute deviation supply a separate grading reference for reports across the case. Explain that anyone, including the nominator, may fund a fixed-score position. Two different funded scores are counterparties, even if neither defends the previous ruling. Keep the bounded admission window open for later proposals. All admitted positions share a later funding deadline. With at least two funded positions, draw the shared jury after admission closes and all admitted funding outcomes are known; if any position is partial, wait for the common deadline to determine funded positions and full-refund entitlements. The appellant may serve if drawn. The calculator illustrates score replacement and omits contribution transfers, jury fees and backer payouts. Discuss inverse-distance settlement and its limits after the simple demonstration, using the [appeal notes](presentation-sources.md#appeal-crowdfunding).
- **Slide 8:** Inspect the allocation table. Effective weight is the lower of requested weight and curated score. Normalizing positive eligible weights produces shares summing to one, independently of a grant budget. Set grant flow to zero: the shares remain while payment rates become zero. Restore the flow to show it acting on those shares. A 3:1 raw weight ratio does not establish three times the public benefit, and binding request caps can change the actual ratio. The initial and appeal views show rates after the new nomination's holdback and the applicable score finalization. Later rating changes do not restart holdback. These are accrual rates, not daily transfers: the beneficiary must claim accrued funds. The inspected contracts release by block; units per day are a readable equivalent.
- **Slide 9:** Introduce the independent accuracy route here for teaching purposes; a challenge need not wait for scoring or completion of a relevance case. Explain the intended nomination-level rule: a successful falsification or non-falsifiability challenge costs that nomination its bond and future pool funding; all previously accrued funding remains owed, including unclaimed amounts. The nominator may submit a fresh bonded nomination under the ordinary rules; the old one remains debunked. Publication is not prior certification of truth, and no additional evidence-approval gate is implied. The intended design permits factual challenges whenever the nomination is active, including before scoring and during relevance proceedings. A withdrawn nomination cannot receive a new challenge. The inspected contracts impose the narrower scored-or-active and no-open-relevance-case conditions; distinguish that implementation from the intended rule. The quoted claim about unchanged binary compatibility is a counterfactual addition, not a claim NumPy made. Its migration guide provides counter-evidence. During a dispute, accrual and earned-funding claims continue. A nomination withdrawal requested after the dispute queues behind it; if the nomination is debunked, that queued exit is cancelled. Withdrawing a nomination and claiming earned funding are separate operations. “Show debunked outcome” illustrates stopped future weight and preserved accrued funding; it does not enact arbitration or calculate a settlement.
- **Slide 10:** Apply the lower re-score and return to the allocation table. An increase or decrease changes future earning when the new score is adopted under the ordinary finalization rules. Holdback applies only to new nominations, not later rating changes. Re-scoring does not guarantee that every stale claim is found or every due evaluation completes. Reset when finished.
- **Slide 11:** State the dependencies: usable evidence, clear policy, meaningful stake, participation, independent scrutiny, adequate arbitration and accessible appeals. A configured temporary circuit breaker can stop future accrual; it cannot take already accrued funds. Contracts enforce procedures and financial consequences; they cannot determine whether the chosen policy serves the public or certify jurors' expertise. A faithfully applied policy can still exclude important needs. Separately accepted claims can also contradict one another; individual challenge outcomes do not establish a consistent body of knowledge.

Participants should be able to distinguish the route for a false factual claim from the route for a disputed relevance judgment, and explain how either can affect funding. Avoid spending this segment on every parameter.

### Worked-example checkpoints

Use these as checkpoints for the default example, not as independent configuration. The [deck's worked-example data](../public/deck/index.html) owns the inputs. The comparison archive and registry are illustrative positions with unchanged weights.

| Action | NumPy score | NumPy accrual equivalent | What to ask |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reset the example | 0.60 | 40 units per day | Why is a score of 0.60 not 60 percent of the flow? |
| Apply appeal result | 0.70 | 43.75 units per day | Which other shares shrink when this weight increases? |
| Apply lower re-score | 0.30 | 25 units per day | Why does the cut affect future accrual without recovering earlier payments? |
| Show debunked outcome | Weight stopped | 0 units per day | Why does future funding stop while all prior earnings remain owed? |

The default grant flow is an illustrative 100 units per day. At the reset state, NumPy's effective weight is 0.60 against a total weight of 1.50. Its share is 0.60 ÷ 1.50 = 40 percent; applying the flow yields 40 units per day. A zero grant flow leaves that share unchanged and yields no earnings. Changing a curator slider returns the calculator to the initial-jury stage; use the named buttons to choose a later outcome. The re-score button restores the original jury before applying the lower assessment. The re-score and debunked buttons show alternative consequences, not a chronological continuation of a debunked nomination. Debunking preserves the recorded relevance score while stopping its earning weight.

For the sole-nomination boundary, explain: “If this pool had only one eligible positive weight, even a score of 0.01 would take the whole allocation share. A later budget uses that share. Other nominations can enter under the normal pool rules and compete for a share.” This is the intended entry incentive, not evidence that entry occurs. In a batch pool, the closed submission window still prevents late entry into that round. With zero total eligible weight, no allocation vector exists and no arbitrary equal allocation is made. Grant funding is optional; nomination bonds, service fees and appeal costs retain their separate rules.

The debunked branch stops future accrual and preserves all prior earnings, whether paid or still unclaimed. The forfeited nomination bond is separate from those earnings. The calculator shows current rates only. It does not simulate balances, actual asset transfers, eligibility checks, jury drawing, arbitration, appeal crowdfunding or settlement. In the working paper, a relevance-appeal chain with an already fully funded contested level continues to settle its backers even if a separate accuracy decision closes the item. That continuation cannot reopen feed or funding eligibility, and it is not simulated by these buttons. If accuracy closure occurs during the still-open initial appeal before any contested level is fully funded, the working paper instead stops admission and funding and refunds all accepted contributions; prior deadline-based finality and already-earned jury fees remain intact. Completed voluntary author withdrawal uses the same threshold where withdrawal is already allowed; requesting withdrawal or waiting through its cooldown does not itself cancel an appeal. The author's existing exit and bond-return rules are unchanged. A separate rule lets an ordinary jury already successfully drawn before either exit finish its historical assessment. A valid ruling earns the normal fee and opens the normal appeal process; the earlier exit cannot cancel that new window retroactively. This cannot restore feed or beneficiary-funding eligibility. A failed attempt completes normal proof and failure settlement without a new score, ETH fee or appeal window. No fresh ordinary retry is drawn for the inactive item; its unused ordinary fee reservation is released through the existing cancellation path. Active-item ordinary retries and protected appeal-chain retries keep their own rules. These proceedings are not simulated by the buttons.

## Join, read the policy and mark the workshop boundary

Before anyone joins, read the shared participation notice: use an alias; aliases, revealed scores and standings are visible to the room; some rounds assign an adversarial role that participants may decline. Watching without joining and sitting a nominee out are ordinary participation choices, not evidence of incompetence. Room records remain stored until the host wipes them; there is no automatic expiry. Wiping room records does not remove browser-stored seat credentials or sealed-score proofs. Ask permission before photographing or sharing identifiable results.

On slide 12, participants who choose to play scan the room code or type the printed address, enter an alias and press “Take a seat”. Compare the seated count with the people joining. Remove duplicate or stray seats with the cross on their chip while still in the lobby.

On slide 13, read the policy, attribution rule, anchors, worked examples and exclusions together. The policy in [the shared domain](../public/domain.js) is authoritative for this workshop: it is one authored example, not a protocol-wide default or mandatory rubric. Each pool crafts its own curation policy. Label the worked scores as hypothetical calibration examples, not measured ratings. Say: “We are keeping this exercise lightweight: use the brief descriptions and what you already know. We have not supplied a common evidence dossier. These scores illustrate coordination under uneven information, rather than a rigorous assessment of research-wide reliance.”

Apply the shared attribution rule to the contribution actually nominated. If two entries claim the same work, identify the shared portion and credit it only once across them; distinct work by the same beneficiary can still earn separate credit. The contract cannot recognize semantic overlap. A deposit and a different registry identifier do not establish distinct work. Do not turn this into a global ban on repeat beneficiaries.

For this example, ask what role the nominee’s completed and maintained work plays in current research workflows and what alternatives are available. Completed work can remain important; completion alone is no reason to discount it. Assess the available current-use evidence without inferring future maintenance or the benefit of another grant. Do not add an unstated funding-need or popularity criterion. Whether need or expected benefit from another grant should count is a choice for this pool's curation policy, not a missing mechanism. Other pools may choose differently; no change to the workshop policy follows from that distinction. Knowledge gaps can justify recusal; disagreeing with others does not by itself mean someone lacks knowledge.

On slide 14, say: “You will try one relevance-curation game with equal seats and points. This hosted game uses a mean and standard deviation, with rewards shared by closeness inside the band. The current full-system contracts use different grading, drawn stake and an appeal ladder. This exercise does not reproduce those contracts.”

Use the widget to show the centre, spread and band. At essentially zero spread, distance forfeits are disabled. Forfeits join the pot. This workshop pays the full base reward for a non-void round. The paper's dispersion-scaled calculation is a single-asset analytical model; the working design pays a fixed ETH service fee per timely, validly revealed seat when a valid ruling is issued, separately from redistribution of utility-token penalties. Each coherent curator's utility-token reward rounds down once on its full original locked weight, with the remainder retained in that pool's separate utility-token balance. A tiny reward pool can yield zero additional tokens for every curator. This does not change the ETH fee; coherence does not reduce that fee. A proven vote leak redirects the violator's fee to its successful reporter. The assigned recipient can withdraw when that jury issues a valid ruling; later appeal score changes do not claw it back. Backer pots follow their separate chain-finality rule. The authoritative wording is `SESSION.workshopReward` in [session data](../public/session.js).

Explain the incentive: jurors anticipate what others applying this policy will judge. That expectation may encourage careful reading, but it can also reward shared bias. Neither agreement nor profit proves improved judgment.

## Open and debrief each round

Before scoring, explain the rules without announcing an expected winner, centre or spread. Keep the privately recorded comparison hidden until every compared round has closed. Afterwards, reveal the original note and compare it with the observed results, including failures. If the expectation was announced early, record that exposure and treat the exercise as primed.

Read dispersion together with the mean, response count, recusals and reasons. Scores bounded by zero and one have less room to vary near an endpoint. A wider band alone does not establish more substantive disagreement, less expertise or worse judgment. These rounds concern different nominees and share participants and discussion; they are not independent trials.

Announce the grading cutoff before pressing “Open”. Reserve the end of the processing window for waking phones, opening seals and grading. “Close and reveal” unlocks when everyone has sealed or recused, or when the scoring clock runs out.

After closing, ask everyone to wake their phone and keep the room open. Connected phones reveal stored seals automatically. Check “Not yet revealed” and allow recovery before the announced cutoff. Do not require every seal to open. A person who never sealed or lost the stored salt cannot reveal. “Grade” fixes the cutoff: later reveals are recorded but excluded from that result.

Read the centre, band, who was paid and who forfeited. Ask:

1. Which policy test or piece of evidence most affected your score?
2. What did the game reward?
3. What would justify changing the judgment or asking another jury?

Then press “Next nominee”. A forfeit does not prove incompetence; a reward does not prove accuracy. If an informed minority loses, discuss why a full system needs a correction path and what that path requires.

On arXiv, say “assigned adversarial role” and “other jurors.” Compare the banner's assigned group with the jurors who submitted its instructed score. Assignment is sampled seat by seat; a majority, compliance and a successful attack are not guaranteed. Role compliance can reflect following instructions. A matching score establishes neither motive nor voluntarily chosen manipulation, and the other jurors are not certified honest by their reports. The outcome shows what these reports earned under this configuration.

## After the demonstration: attacks, responses and limits

On slide 16, inspect the actual result and widget. If the bloc benefits, ask what controlled the reference. If it loses, ask which scores, stake distribution and band produced that outcome. Neither result establishes a universal safe collusion threshold.

Keep the live demonstration focused on the scoring rules and observed results. Explain proof reporting when participants need to use it, without presenting it as general protection against collusion. Introduce the attack catalogue on slide 17, after participants have seen the mechanism operate.

For each row on slide 17, state the attack, the response and its remaining weakness:

- **Different criteria:** a published policy supplies a shared rubric and evidence requirements. Ambiguity, a poor policy and shared bias remain possible.
- **Copying or coordination:** commit and reveal binds each reveal to a prior commitment. The inspected contract does not require commitments to close before reveals begin: one juror can reveal while others are still able to commit. It therefore does not fully prevent copying, private coordination or payment after a public reveal.
- **Jury capture:** stake-backed draws put selected capital at risk. Its distribution and value determine how much protection it supplies; it does not certify expertise.
- **A disputed result:** permissionless public score positions can fund a shared appeal jury. Two different fully funded scores oppose one another; no separate defense of the old score is required. Admission stays open for its full bounded window, provisionally 72 elapsed hours, so two fast funders cannot close it early. Every admitted position gets the full common funding interval after admission closes, provisionally 72 elapsed hours, and may also fund earlier. Both cutoffs are exclusive: a new position or contribution included exactly at its respective deadline is too late. The next appeal targets twice the previous ruling's planned seats, regardless of a smaller actual draw; retries keep that target. The jury waits for known funding outcomes; fully funded groups need no extra delay, while the group waits for any position that remains underfunded. At the common deadline, positions still below target cancel. If none fully funds, the referenced ruling becomes final then, partial contributions are refundable in full without a new jury fee, and earlier adjudicated pots settle against that ruling's issued score. Refund collection cannot delay finality. Only one ordinary round per item and pool may be scheduled or running at a time, across revisions. Where ordinary reassessment recurs, its next round becomes eligible one full cadence after the last applicable hold ends; missed rounds do not accumulate, ordinary funding and eligibility still apply, and a new hold resets the wait. One fully funded distinct score wins by forfeiture only after admission closes and all admitted funding outcomes are known; its principal returns in full without a jury fee or profit on that position, and the result is procedural rather than consensus. All funded positions against the ruling combine in one common pot; pool funds pay the jury first and that pot covers the shortfall once. Fresh eligible fee reservations receive available pool funds in successful execution order, without displacing existing reservations or changing the ordinary scheduler’s order. Earlier filing alone reserves nothing. Faster inclusion can obtain the subsidy and leave an unreserved ordinary round awaiting funds; later deposits do not revise an earlier funding split. At the ultimate end of the appeal chain, each distinct position receives a normalized weight equal to the reciprocal of its absolute score error; a unique exact prediction receives the whole net pot. Each position pays its backers pro rata. This reduces to the established two-position split and applies to larger groups. Backers proposing the same numeric score against the same ruling contribute to one existing position under its cap and clock, with pro rata payouts. Proposals use 0.01 increments from 0 to 1; off-grid proposals are rejected, while jury-report and final-score precision remain unchanged. Payouts use the actual final score. A closer position can still lose principal. Where edits are allowed, an update does not erase a pending relevance appeal: the appeal remains on the earlier revision, and its outcome stays in that revision's history. The new revision waits for its own adopted score before score-dependent ranking or new score-dependent funding. It stays accessible through direct links or pending views and cannot inherit the old score even provisionally. Beneficiary funding already accrued under the earlier revision remains attributed to it and immediately claimable. An edit neither erases that balance nor transfers it to a newer revision. Position allocations round down to whole wei, with the remainder credited once at chain finality to the same pool's ETH jury-service budget. Unclaimed payouts remain assigned to their recipients. Backer allocations use cumulative rounding in funding-acceptance order, distributing the entire position allocation independently of claim order. Interleaved funding can affect rounding gains. Anyone may trigger an already-claimable ETH jury fee, appeal payout or contribution refund to the original recipient's current standing receiving address, without changing ownership, amount or unlock conditions. Only the original recipient may change or reset the address; each payment uses the setting active when it executes, covering unpaid and future payments while completed transfers stay final. Failed transfers leave unpaid claims reserved and retryable. Update interfaces, replay-protection representation, remaining transfer mechanics, other monetary rounding and the incentives to fund adjacent distinct proposals need further hardening. Use the [source register](presentation-sources.md#appeal-crowdfunding) for the frozen target, locks, full-chain finality, 80% weight quorum and unlimited fee-free quorum retries. Neither crowdfunding nor a larger jury guarantees correction or completion.
- **Proof of a sealed score:** during scoring, another seated juror can report a valid proof in this workshop, triggering a penalty and bounty. A buyer can instead use a public revealed score or another verification channel. This is not general protection against vote buying.

The workshop's proof-reporting bounty is an extra demonstration feature. No corresponding commitment-leak reporting or penalty path was found in the inspected full-system contracts. Keep that scope attached to the response when discussing it.

## Arrow, evidence and the close

On slide 18, return to the full map and highlight the part Arrow explores. Invited experts and trusted nominations replace open entry, and Arrow omits accuracy disputes, drawn staked juries, relevance appeals and continuing funding. It is a precursor, not a miniature validation of the complete system. Use the current follow-up wording from `SESSION.nextRound` in [session data](../public/session.js).

On slide 19, distinguish evidence types and use the [evaluation plan](evaluation-plan.md) for the next research steps. The October 11 manuscript formalizes a mechanism and reports conditional calculations, simulations under prescribed behavior and a partial historical deployment. The current contracts implement a different profile. This workshop supplies observations and questions from one room. Arrow can test participation, prompt comprehension, scoring patterns and operations under its assumptions. Permissionless security, appeal behavior and integrated funding still require further evidence. Test the private-to-public link by examining the behavior participants choose, the information and decisions it produces, and feasible alternatives. Serving a public purpose also requires examining whose needs the policy includes, who can afford to contest a result, and what funded work actually delivers.

Describe the next study as a proposed evaluation, not an approved or completed Arrow protocol. Comprehension needs independently scored cases; updating needs new evidence and a comparison; public benefit needs a bounded decision, affected people, observable consequences and all process costs. Stable scores and profitable jurors do not substitute for those measures. Before any efficacy study, fix the population, primary outcome, comparison, horizon and decision criteria using task relevance and pilot uncertainty, then preserve unfavorable results.

Ask: “Whose needs are missing from this policy? What evidence would show that the resulting funding served its stated purpose?” Then ask which of those claims the available evidence supports. Do not treat every possible outcome as support.

On slide 20, return to public benefit as the final aim and credible consensus as an intermediate objective. Ask one participant to distinguish accuracy challenge from relevance appeal, and another to explain what would make an agreed judgment credible or justify revising it. Then ask what evidence would show that the resulting decisions benefited affected people. A profitable strategy, faithful policy application or stable consensus alone would not answer that question. Close with the full loop, research questions and paper/mailing-list links. Press “Finish” if needed so standings remain visible.

## While the room plays

- “Sit this nominee out” recuses a juror: no scoring stake, reward share or absence fine. A prior proof leak can still be penalized. “Take my seat back” restores scoring while it is open. Nobody has to claim a knowledge deficit to decline.
- Jurors may change a seal until closing. The chip marks a changed seal with a small arrow.
- A proof contains a score and two words. Another juror seated for that nominee can submit it through “Report” for a bounty; the disclosing juror loses more. This concerns proof disclosure, not discussion of the policy. “Proofs: on screen” can put proofs behind a button.
- A phone waking before “Grade” can reveal automatically. A non-recused seated juror without a timely reveal loses the stake if at least one score opens. If nobody reveals, the round is void with no absence forfeits.
- Late arrivals may join any time and are at stake from the next nominee opened.

## Recovery and paper fallback

If the laptop drops, open the room on the phone, press “Take over” and confirm. If a screen says the game moved on, check the current state: another host action may already have succeeded. “End the game” keeps completed results. “New game” returns everyone to the summon screen and reseats them.

If everyone's connection fails, use the [score sheets](../public/paper/sheets.html). Jurors write in the nominee's row and fold the sheet. At “open”, read scores aloud in seat order and enter them in the [paper calculator](../public/paper/index.html) on a connected device, or later. The same ledger grades them.

Keep aliases consistent and enter a score or explicit recusal for each juror in each played round. An omitted name is an absence if at least one score opens. The calculator treats every name entered anywhere as seated in every entered round. For late arrivals, enter “recuse” in earlier rounds and explain the workaround. Leave unplayed rounds blank.

Paper play has no private bloc instruction. Everyone scores arXiv under the shared policy; use slide 16 for the collusion discussion. If switching after live rounds, announce that paper standings restart from this point: earlier balances are not imported. Allow extra collection time and shorten discussion explicitly.

If a stranger joins, remove their seat in the lobby. The room link is public; do not promise stronger access control.

## After the session

Explain that final standings remain visible until the host wipes them. Do not invite identifiable photographs without the people concerned agreeing. After the closing discussion, press “Wipe all” and confirm. The room has no automatic expiry; verify the wipe rather than assuming the session timeout removed data. Server-side seat authentication and browser-stored credentials and proofs remain after a room wipe; photographs already taken cannot be erased by it.

Keep only notes covered by the participation notice and the people concerned's consent. Prefer non-identifying observations about policy interpretations, surprising scores, minority reasoning, assigned roles and observed compliance. Further research use or retention of identifiable material needs separate disclosure and consent. Separate observations from explanations. This room did not test the entire protocol.

Follow the [README](../README.md) for authorized mailing-list exports and secret handling. Keep tokens out of slides, shared notes and handouts.
