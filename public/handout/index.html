Who judges what matters?

Curating public goods requires judging both factual claims and relevance to a shared purpose. How can those judgments earn trust when participants have their own interests? Decentralized Curation aims for credible judgment without trusting individual participants or requiring permission to take part.

Curating public goods

  1. Set the policy. Define what matters, what evidence is needed and how assessors are rewarded.
  2. Nominate work. Anyone can identify a contribution, publish claims and evidence, and put down a deposit. Claims can enter before being fact-checked.
  3. Judge relevance. People lock tokens they risk losing to enter the jury lottery. Selected jurors score independently under the policy.
  4. Keep scores current. Renew scores periodically. Anyone can fund an objection; competing funded proposals trigger an appeal with a larger jury.
  5. Publish the assessment. Express how much each eligible contribution matters relative to the others under the policy.
  6. Challenge facts at any time. Anyone can challenge an active nomination’s claims. An external arbitrator decides. A false or untestable claim closes that nomination and forfeits its deposit. A fresh nomination is allowed.
