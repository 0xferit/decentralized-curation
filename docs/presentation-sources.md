# Presentation evidence register

This workshop explains the full decentralized-curation mechanism and its aim: make useful information production, assessment and correction privately rewarding so that the resulting decisions serve the public. Credible consensus is an intermediate objective, not proof of public benefit. The live exercise isolates one relevance-scoring step; Arrow is an exploratory experiment toward the fuller system. Neither supplies evidence that the integrated mechanism is secure or benefits affected people.

## Judgment and public accountability

The opening asks how private rewards can support useful assessment and correction, and how that work can serve affected people. The [current manuscript](https://github.com/0xferit/manuscript-decentralized-curation/blob/9927927cebd11f3b4e66f80bebee65b3248af919/paper.qmd#L27-L41) requires evidence for both links. Public participation can reveal needs and contribute knowledge; expert or delegated assessment can itself be collective. This is a design aim, not a demonstrated advantage of monarchy over democracy. These sources inform the framing, not claims about the implemented contracts.

| Primary source | Supported framing | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| John Dewey, [*The Public and Its Problems* (1927)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/71000/pg71000-images.html), pp. 206–209 | Expertise can become disconnected from the needs it should serve without public voice. Discussion helps reveal those needs and judge the bearing of expert knowledge. This is the closest source for combining informed judgment with public accountability. | Dewey does not reduce democracy to vote-counting or establish that experts should unilaterally choose public ends. This is a political argument, not validation of the funding mechanism. |
| Elinor Ostrom, [“Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems” (2010)](https://web.pdx.edu/~nwallace/EHP/OstromPolyGov.pdf), pp. 641–644, 652–653 | Her institutional research examines combinations of decision centers rather than one universal organizational form. Durable resource arrangements often include affected users in rulemaking, accountable monitoring and accessible conflict resolution. | The findings concern particular institutions and resource settings. They do not show that this protocol represents the public, makes appeals affordable or guarantees beneficial outcomes. |
| Lu Hong and Scott E. Page, [“Groups of diverse problem solvers can outperform groups of high-ability problem solvers” (2004)](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/scottepage/wp-content/uploads/sites/344/2015/11/pnas.pdf) | Their formal model gives conditions under which diversity of problem-solving approaches can outweigh higher individual performance. Participation can contribute information as well as express preferences. | The result requires capable participants, a suitable problem and the model's other assumptions. It does not prove that any crowd beats experts, or resolve disagreement about public values. |

For this mechanism, the creator states the policy, jurors assess contributions, and wider participants can supply evidence or pay to pursue eligible challenges and appeals. The resulting weights govern allocation. The contracts enforce specified procedures and financial consequences. Whether the policy serves affected people, whether assessors are competent, and whether correction paths are usable remain separate questions to investigate. Publishing a policy is not democratic authorization; stake is not proof of expertise. The manuscript [makes those limits explicit](https://github.com/0xferit/manuscript-decentralized-curation/blob/9927927cebd11f3b4e66f80bebee65b3248af919/paper.qmd#L686-L694): public benefit is an objective, not an additional quality score, and exit has not been shown equivalent to effective public participation.

The release-note link uses the later 2.2 manual's historical 2.0.0 notes. The 2.0 manual's copy retains a stale “release in progress” banner; the announcement and package record establish the actual release date. This choice follows the relevant content update rather than treating a page's crawl time as an event date.

NumPy's nomination, requested weight, curator identities, ticket counts, scores, comparison positions and 100-unit daily release equivalent are illustrative. The initial six-ticket mean, twelve-ticket appeal and later re-score drive a working allocation calculation; they are not reported evaluations of NumPy. Initial and appeal rates represent an active allocation after finalization and holdback. Funds accrue for the beneficiary to claim; these figures are not scheduled daily transfers. The calculator omits balances, asset transfers, eligibility enforcement and contract settlement.

The accuracy branch adds a deliberately counterfactual assertion that existing NumPy 1.x compiled extensions work unchanged. NumPy did not make that claim in this example. The migration guide supplies the counter-evidence. The button illustrates an external arbitrator rejecting that added claim after scoring and closure of the relevance case; no real accuracy ruling occurs. Debunking stops future weight and returns unclaimed accrual to the open pool without recovering prior payments. The lower re-score is a separate alternative outcome, not an action after debunking.

## Claims the evidence does not support

- **No guarantee that all commitments precede all reveals.** In the inspected [commit and reveal functions](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/src/CoherenceEngine.sol#L939-L975), committing requires a block before the commit deadline, while revealing only requires a block before the reveal deadline and a matching prior commitment. Early reveals can be observed while other jurors can still commit. This is a source-inspection finding, not a reproduced exploit or a claim about a deployed system.

- **No general honesty theorem or safe collusion threshold.** The current paper gives a profitable unilateral deviation and explains why selected simulation points do not identify a safe threshold: [lines 372–396](https://github.com/0xferit/manuscript-decentralized-curation/blob/9927927cebd11f3b4e66f80bebee65b3248af919/paper.qmd#L372-L396). The implementation's own [majority-bloc test, lines 446–476](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/test/unit/CaseSettlement.t.sol#L446-L476) shows the honest minority being penalized.
- **No established public-benefit optimum.** The manuscript specifies no public-welfare objective function and proves no optimum. Policy fidelity, participant profits and equilibrium would not by themselves establish the best attainable outcome: [lines 668–670](https://github.com/0xferit/manuscript-decentralized-curation/blob/9927927cebd11f3b4e66f80bebee65b3248af919/paper.qmd#L668-L670).
- **No guarantee of consistency across claims.** Separate challenge outcomes do not establish that all retained claims can be true together. The protocol specifies no cross-item consistency test: [lines 672–674](https://github.com/0xferit/manuscript-decentralized-curation/blob/9927927cebd11f3b4e66f80bebee65b3248af919/paper.qmd#L672-L674).
- **No verified production deployment or completed audit of this integrated implementation.** This review establishes source content, not operational status. The paper reports [a partial accuracy-layer deployment in 2023](https://github.com/0xferit/manuscript-decentralized-curation/blob/9927927cebd11f3b4e66f80bebee65b3248af919/paper.qmd#L23); that is a different claim.
- **No unlimited-jury guarantee.** No configured appeal-depth ceiling exists, but the implementation documents [a finite seat-field operating bound](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/src/CoherenceEngine.sol#L291-L311).
- **No automatic elimination of trust.** Pool terms disclose the assets, arbitrator and paymaster. A configured temporary circuit breaker is an [express fairness exception](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/packages/contracts/src/Pool.sol#L1734-L1763).
- **No implemented juror leak penalty established here.** The workshop's disclosure bounty and the news blueprint's proposed reporting path must not be attributed to the inspected Solidity engine.
- **No implemented curator stipend in this release.** It is [explicitly deferred](https://github.com/golemfoundation/octant-v3-monorepo/blob/ca8c8560119dee34f809a31e5149f4a6217467fb/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-15-dispute-coherence-design.md#L71-L77).

For facilitation: present the loop first, identify what the exercise isolates, then distinguish observations from hypotheses. Agreement, disagreement, a successful appeal example or a smooth workshop run each answers a narrower question than whether the full system works.


## Preparation method

The narrative and visual review used the scientific-slides workflow from [Scientific Agent Skills: A Library of Procedural Knowledge for Research Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00065), by Timothy Kassis and colleagues (2026). This is a preparation-method reference, not evidence for the curation mechanism.
