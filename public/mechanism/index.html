The intended mechanism

The aim is to reward useful information production, assessment and correction so that decisions serve affected people. Agreement and participant profit are intermediate observations; neither establishes truth or public benefit.

Policy and nominations

Each pool authors its own policy and evidence requirements. A nomination identifies the contribution, beneficiary and supporting claims and posts its required bond. Ordinary publication does not require prior semantic approval. Factual challenges may begin at any time while the nomination is active, including before relevance scoring and during a relevance assessment or appeal; a withdrawn nomination cannot receive a new challenge. A successful challenge establishing falsity or non-falsifiability debunks that nomination, forfeits its bond and stops its future funding. Previously accrued funding remains owed to its beneficiary, including unclaimed amounts. Its author can submit a fresh nomination under the ordinary rules. A nomination withdrawal requested after an accuracy dispute enters the event queue behind that dispute; if the nomination is debunked, that queued withdrawal is cancelled. This does not restrict claims on earned funding.

A contract can check identifiers and financial conditions. It cannot establish that two descriptions are different work or that a policy serves the public. A pool needs an explicit attribution policy for overlap; shared beneficiaries alone do not imply duplicate contributions.

Relevance and jury payment

Jurors stake a dedicated utility token to signal availability and receive a chance of being drawn. Stake and seats do not certify competence or independent judgment. Jurors assess a pinned nomination and policy. When quorum is met, the weighted mean of valid reports supplies the policy-relative relevance assessment; agreement-based grading can still reward shared error or punish informed dissent.

The intended design separates assets: ether (ETH) pays jury service and backs financial positions; the utility token supplies jury collateral and redistribution of incoherence penalties. Valid service fees are paid per eligible revealed seat only when the jury issues a valid ruling. Failed quorum creates no service fee. Later appeals do not claw back earned service fees. A proven leak can redirect the violator's fee and actual confiscated utility tokens to its reporter. These are intended rules, not behavior demonstrated by the hosted points game.

Publicly funded appeals

Anyone may fund a fixed-score appeal position, including a nominator, appellant or juror. Supporting the same score contributes to the same position. Different fully funded scores compete in one shared jury and pot after the bounded admission and funding opportunities finish. The next jury has more planned seats; more seats alone do not establish more independent information.

Jury costs are deducted according to the funding rules. After the whole appeal chain ends, each adjudicated level settles against its ultimate final score: closer positive-error positions receive larger inverse-distance shares, and a unique exact position receives the net pot. A closer position can still lose principal. A sole funded position wins procedurally by forfeiture, receives its contributions back without a jury fee or gain on that position, and does not establish consensus. Retries and successive appeals can prolong capital lockup; crowdfunding does not establish equal access or guaranteed correction.

Curated weights first; funding can follow

Curation produces policy-relative weights without requiring a beneficiary funding budget. Effective weight is the lower of requested weight and adopted score. Divide eligible weights by their positive total to obtain a vector of allocation shares that sums to one. Any available or later funding budget can then act on those shares. If there are no positive eligible weights, there is no allocation. This does not remove nomination bonds, jury-service fees or appeal funding requirements. Only new nominations wait through holdback before earning. Later rating increases and decreases change future earning when adopted under the ordinary finalization rules, without a new holdback. Funding already earned is immediately claimable, including during an accuracy dispute. The workshop's worked calculator caps weight by the request and divides the available flow proportionally. A positive sole eligible weight can receive the full distributable flow. Weight ratios are allocation choices, not measured ratios of public benefit. New assessments and factual challenges have different consequences; funding already accrued remains owed, whether already paid or still unclaimed.

Voluntary fork recovery

Any group may propose replacement contracts and a utility token using its own exclusion list without proving misconduct or obtaining the old majority's permission. Included holders retain token units from a specified pre-incident snapshot; suspected attackers may be excluded. Exclusion is that group's judgment, so innocent holders can be excluded and attackers can remain included. Users decide whether to adopt the replacement.

The selected recovery design starts with fresh assessments and supports optional conditional funding commitments. Each funder may migrate their remaining attributed contribution where the source pool actually implements that right. Existing liabilities and reserved funds stay protected. Common protocol income remains communal. A snapshot creates no spendable ETH, cannot reverse completed payments and does not give new contracts access to old funds. Recovery tooling and rehearsal remain work to do.

What the workshop actually does

The hosted game uses equal point stakes, sealed reports, a mean and standard-deviation band, and points shared by closeness. It does not execute token staking, funding allocation, accuracy adjudication, public appeal crowdfunding or fork recovery. Participants trust the host and service. The worked funding calculator is an illustration with stipulated inputs, not an on-chain transaction.

This workshop's policy

Use brief descriptions and prior knowledge. No shared evidence dossier is supplied. The scores demonstrate coordination under uneven information, not a rigorous estimate of research-wide reliance.

The adversarial round assigns a role. Its outcome shows submitted reports and resulting payoffs in that configuration, not how often participants would freely choose manipulation. A matching score does not establish motive. More agreement, a wider band or a larger jury does not by itself establish better judgment.

What evidence would count

The following are proposed tests, not completed findings or the full protocol of the Arrow experiment. Before an efficacy study, specify its population, primary outcome, comparison, horizon and practically meaningful success criteria. Do not infer success from agreement or profit alone.

Different claims need different measurements
ClaimMeasure and comparisonReason to revise the claim
ComprehensionIndependently scored unseen rule and policy cases, compared with ordinary guidance.Persistent errors despite confident reporting.
Reliable assessmentRepeated unchanged-evidence cases and separate changed-evidence cases; compare an independent rating process.Instability, anchoring or ignored decisive evidence.
Useful incentivesBlind assessment of reasons and factual support, comparing independently chosen strategies with fixed compensation.More agreement or profit without better evidence use.
Usable correctionAudit challenged and unchallenged items; compare errors, access, delays and all costs with feasible review.Errors persist because investigation or correction is unaffordable.
Public benefitAffected people and accountable funders define a bounded purpose; compare consequences and total costs with a feasible allocation alternative.No meaningful advantage after costs, harmful distribution or failure on the declared purpose.

Jury members affect one another's payoffs. Independent groups, not individual seats in one jury, supply the replication for comparisons of incentives. Independent reviewers can also err: publish their criteria, conflicts, disagreements and uncertainty.

Sources and inspection limits

  • Published working paper: an archived research version. Later local hardening is not part of that published artifact.
  • Source for the published paper: check its revision and release before attributing any rule to it. This summary describes intended choices, not a release announcement.
  • Workshop presentation and shared workshop policy and docket: inspectable teaching material; the points ledger defines the exercise's arithmetic.

The presentation's contract comparison came from an access-controlled implementation snapshot inspected during review. This page neither republishes that code nor makes its behavior independently reproducible. It does not establish that a later deployment matches either that snapshot or the current intended rules.

The Arrow description covers an invited-participant experiment with trusted nominations. Its complete private protocol was not inspected for this review. Participation and scoring observations under those assumptions cannot validate permissionless security, the complete appeal lifecycle or integrated funding.
